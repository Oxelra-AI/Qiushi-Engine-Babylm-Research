# Register Substitution and Mixed-Dose Cancellation

Status: completed matched-pool construction; register-arm scores were not yet available.

Broad compact-companion specificity had weakened: compact-minus-breadth exEntity5 was -0.1694 over 10M-80M and +0.0503 over 80M-100M; the RoBERTa MAX view-minus-clean contrast was negative outside Entity. The remaining question concerned the text displaced by a fixed-budget substitution.

The mixed quarter-dose arm admitted 105,962 FineWeb words while removing 58,922 child/subtitle words, 38,240 Gutenberg/SimpleWiki words and 8,800 other words. A near-zero mixed contrast could represent weak admission or cancellation of different removal costs.

The direct register pair admitted the identical 758-row, 105,962-word FineWeb multiset, rho 0.0105962, in a 65,313-row 10M pool and 653,130-row 100M stream. Child/subtitle-focused removal comprised 99,727 child/subtitle, 3,726 adult-prose and 2,509 other words. Adult-prose-focused removal comprised 84,408 adult-prose, 13,278 child/subtitle and 8,276 other words.

Position matching covered 758 row pairs: mean absolute distance 38.03, median 17, p90 55 and maximum 842; only 3 pairs necessarily overlapped the same clean row. Mean row indices were 3977.3 and 3992.5.

The planned primary readout was the common 10M-to-80M trajectory, direct child-minus-adult contrast and each arm versus matched clean references. Similar positive arms would favor admission effects; diverging arms would reveal removal opportunity cost. Adult-removal superiority would imply value in retaining child/subtitle text; the opposite sign would reject that simple preservation rule. A mixed null with divergent register arms would be cancellation, not absence of substitution effects. Entity operation strata remained separate from broad cheap6/exEntity5 conclusions.
