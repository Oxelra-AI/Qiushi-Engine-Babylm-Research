# Boundaries of the Mechanistic Claims

Scientific status: corrections to interpretation, not changes to experiments or scores.

The evidence combines natural-corpus interventions with controlled mechanism studies. Neither source should be described as the whole evidence base. The resulting design principles are mechanism-informed heuristics, not three universal necessary conditions.

Dense masking removes detected second-view content groups while leaving the source view available. This makes cross-view correspondence more consequential by suppressing within-view completion cues. It does not prove that the model can predict only through the original source: residual context, learned priors, and other prediction paths remain possible. The stronger exclusive-source wording was withdrawn.

Likewise, a useful design objective is to remove cheaper sufficient predictors that would otherwise absorb learning. It is not an established theorem that the intended relation must always be the single cheapest sufficient explanation. Candidate presence, position, operation counts, and format can each provide alternate solutions, which is why controls must test the specific proposed computation.

Ordinary continuation is not a teacher-matched version of acquisition. Ordinary-state and dense-state preservation differ in the prediction states under constraint, including evidence availability and selected positions; the change is not merely an interchangeable loss label. Identical nominal coefficients do not ensure matched effective constraint strength.

The common-support probe is a mechanism fingerprint complementary to source readouts. It does not by itself distinguish cross-view computation from memorization. Its dense-state fits use familiar training rows. Full evaluation and held-out transfer remain separate evidence requirements.

For these reasons the defensible conclusion is selective acquisition and partial preservation on the tested states. Unrestricted transfer, universal necessity, and a uniquely identified teacher mechanism are not established.
