# Balanced State Evidence and Signed Transfer

Status: corrected two-condition probe initiated; no result was available in this design record.

An earlier binary disambiguation design confounded initial-owner pattern with static slot. Even a null result could not resolve independently varied experience when a static-slot shortcut remained available.

The repaired fixed-total comparison used `replace_k00_spread` versus `replace_k16_spread`. In k0, initial and final owners were always opposite, so anti-copy and the event rule agreed. In k16, half the worlds had equal initial and final owners, balanced within every relation, voice and static-slot cell. Copy-initial, anti-copy and static-slot-switch rules failed on held cells while the event rule remained valid.

The probe compared aligned-state bridge, inverted-state bridge and held-held-only arms, with seeds 28400, 28401 and 28402, 40 epochs, training batch 4 and evaluation batch 16. It was a small classification fine-tuning test, not a BabyLM-scale or official endpoint experiment.

Interpretation first required fitting supervised rows. Without fit, failure could reflect optimization rather than learnability. State success required both same-initial and opposite-initial changed cases, including off-diagonal same/slot-0 and opposite/slot-1 cells, while retaining unchanged facts. Paired both-correct accuracy was central: gains on changed rows bought by damage to unchanged rows did not demonstrate selective updating.

Reusable transfer additionally required opposite movement from aligned and inverted evidence on unbridged mixed held-seen comparisons. State improvement with chance mixed orientation would establish only a local event/state rule. Only joint state conservation and signed transfer justified a later low-k information-efficiency experiment; neither a broad k sweep nor mechanism claims followed from aggregate state accuracy alone.
